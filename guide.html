Leads before dial

Put the red lead in the right jack for the job, then turn the dial. Never turn the dial while the probes touch a live circuit.

Power off for Ω

Resistance, continuity, diode and capacitance only work on dead circuits. Discharge capacitors first.

Current goes in series

Before trusting a "0 V" reading on something you'll touch, check the meter on a known live source, then again after.

V

Voltage (DC and AC)

Measures the push behind a circuit, from batteries and USB rails up to wall outlets.

10AmACOMVΩHz

Red in VΩHz, black in COM. Probes go across the thing you're measuring, in parallel.

Use it for

  • Battery health (a "9 V" battery under 7.5 V is spent)
  • Power supplies, USB 5 V, 3.3 V logic rails
  • Car electrical: 12.6 V resting, about 14 V running
  • Outlets and appliance supply (AC)

How to

  1. Turn the dial to V.
  2. Press FUNC to choose DC or AC. The screen shows DC or AC.
  3. Touch black to ground or negative first, then red to the test point.
  4. A minus sign means the red probe is on the more negative side.

Careful

  • Limits: 1000 V DC and 750 V AC. Don't measure anything that could exceed them.
  • Keep your fingers behind the finger guards on the probes.
  • On mains, the meter and leads must be rated for the place you're measuring. See safety ratings.

mV

Millivolts

Finer resolution for signals under 600 mV. It still autoranges within that span.

10AmACOMVΩHz

Same jacks as volts.

Use it for

  • Sensor outputs (strain gauges, current-sense amps, thermocouple voltage)
  • Voltage across a current-shunt resistor
  • Voltage drop across a switch, relay contact or connector. Anything over about 100 mV on a closed contact means a bad joint.

How to

  1. Turn the dial to mV and press FUNC for DC or AC.
  2. Connect across the small signal.
  3. If you see OL, the signal is over 600 mV. Switch to V.

Tip

Start unknown voltages in V. Use mV once you know the signal is small.

Hz%

Frequency and duty cycle

How fast a signal repeats (Hz) and what share of each cycle it is high (%).

10AmACOMVΩHz

Black to the signal's ground, red to the signal.

Use it for

  • PWM from a microcontroller (fan, LED dimming, servo)
  • Clock and oscillator outputs
  • Fan tachometer pulses, signal generators

How to

  1. Turn the dial to Hz%.
  2. Connect to the signal. The screen shows the frequency.
  3. Press Hz% to switch to duty cycle in %, and press again to switch back.

Careful

  • This position is for low-voltage signals. To check mains frequency, use V on AC and press Hz%.
  • Noisy or slow edges give jumpy readings. Probe close to the source and keep the ground short.

Resistance, continuity, diode, capacitance

Four tests share this position. Press FUNC to step through them in this order: Ω → continuity → diode → capacitance.

10AmACOMVΩHz

Red in VΩHz, black in COM. The meter supplies its own small test voltage.

Careful, for all four

  • The circuit must be switched off. Any outside voltage gives wrong readings and can damage the meter.
  • Discharge capacitors before testing them or anything near them. Large ones can hold a dangerous charge.
Ω

Resistance

Use it for

  • Checking resistor values, pots and thermistors
  • Heater elements, motor and relay coils
  • Fuses (good ≈ 0 Ω, blown = OL)

How to

  1. Touch the probes across the part. Polarity doesn't matter.
  2. OL means open or above range. Autorange steps through Ω, kΩ and MΩ.
  3. For low values, touch the probe tips together, press REL, then measure. This subtracts the leads' own resistance.

Tip

Don't pinch both probe tips with your fingers on high values: your body adds a parallel path. Readings taken in-circuit are often low because of other parts in parallel. Lift one leg of the part for a true value.

•)))

Continuity

Use it for

  • Finding which wire goes where in a cable or harness
  • Checking switches, fuses and PCB traces
  • Hunting shorts between power and ground

How to

  1. Press FUNC once from Ω. The •))) symbol appears.
  2. Touch the probes across the path. A beep means a low-resistance connection.
▶|

Diode

Use it for

  • Checking diodes, LEDs and rectifier bridges
  • Transistor junctions (B–E and B–C act like diodes)
  • Finding a diode's polarity

Reading it

  • Red on anode, black on cathode (the band side). The meter shows the forward voltage.
  • Silicon diodes read about 0.5–0.7 V, Schottky diodes 0.15–0.45 V, LEDs about 1.6–3.3 V (they may glow faintly).
  • Reversed: OL. Low both ways means shorted. OL both ways means open.
⊣⊢

Capacitance

Use it for

  • Checking electrolytic caps that have dried out (reading well under the printed value)
  • Identifying unmarked ceramic or film caps

How to

  1. Discharge the capacitor first.
  2. Press FUNC until the unit shows F (nF, µF, mF).
  3. Electrolytics: red to +. Wait: large values take several seconds to settle.
  4. For small values, press REL with the leads apart first to cancel the leads' own capacitance.
Temp

Temperature

Reads a K-type thermocouple probe, the bead-on-a-wire probe that comes with the meter.

10AmACOMVΩHz

Probe + (red) into the jack marked Temp, − into COM.

Use it for

  • Heatsinks, motors and regulators running hot
  • 3D-printer beds, ovens, water baths
  • Room and outdoor air

How to

  1. Turn the dial to Temp and plug in the probe.
  2. Press °C/°F to pick units.
  3. Hold the bead firmly against the surface and give it a few seconds to settle.

Careful

  • The meter reads up to 1000 °C, but the supplied bead probe is usually rated much lower, often around 200–250 °C. Check the probe's own rating.
  • The thermocouple tip is bare metal. Never touch it to anything live.

hFE

Transistor gain

Checks a bipolar transistor's current gain (β), for sorting and matching parts.

10AmACOMVΩHz

No test leads. Plug the hFE adapter into the two left jacks, which are bracketed hFE on the panel.

How to

  1. Remove the test leads and plug in the adapter.
  2. Turn the dial to hFE.
  3. Look up the transistor's pinout. Insert it into the NPN or PNP row with E, B and C in the matching holes.
  4. Read the gain. Typical small-signal parts read 100–400.

Good to know

  • Out-of-circuit parts only.
  • It tests at one small current, so use it to compare parts, not as a full spec.
  • A reading near 0 usually means wrong pin order or the wrong NPN/PNP row.

µA

Microamps

For tiny currents, like what a device draws while it sleeps.

10AmACOMVΩHz

Red moves to the mA jack. The meter goes in series: break the circuit and let the current flow through the meter.

Use it for

  • Sleep and standby current of ESP32, Arduino and battery gadgets
  • Leakage current, photodiodes, tiny sensors

How to measure any current

  1. Power the circuit off.
  2. Move red to mA (or 10A), then set the dial.
  3. Open the circuit (e.g. lift the battery + wire) and bridge the gap with the probes.
  4. Power on and read. Press FUNC for AC.
  5. Power off, remove the meter, and put red back in VΩHz.

Tip

The meter drops a little voltage while measuring current, more on µA than on mA. If a device resets when it wakes from sleep, measure on mA instead.

mA

Milliamps

Everyday electronics currents, up to 600 mA.

10AmACOMVΩHz

Red in mA, in series. This jack is protected by a 600 mA fuse.

Use it for

  • LED current (typically 5–20 mA)
  • Dev boards running: Arduino ~20–50 mA, ESP32 ~80–250 mA with Wi-Fi on
  • 4–20 mA industrial sensor loops

Good to know

  • Over 600 mA blows the fuse. If unsure, start on A with the 10A jack.
  • Reads 0 when you're sure current is flowing? Check the fuse (see fuse check).

Careful, for all current modes

  • Never put the probes across a voltage source (battery, power supply, outlet). The meter is a near-short in this mode. Best case it blows the fuse. On mains it can cause an arc flash.
  • Always move the red lead back to VΩHz when you're done. Forgetting this is how most meters get damaged.

A

Amps

High currents up to 10 A.

10AmACOMVΩHz

Red in 10A, in series. This jack has its own 10 A fuse.

Use it for

  • Motors, pumps, heaters and fans
  • Battery chargers and LED strips
  • Car accessories and parasitic drain (start on A, then step down)

Good to know

  • Keep readings above about 5 A short, and let the meter cool between them. The internal shunt heats up.
  • Your test leads and clips must be rated for the current too.
  • Motors draw several times their running current at start-up. Connect first, then switch on.

Careful

Same rules as mA: series only, never across a source, and put the red lead back in VΩHz afterwards.

Reference

Reading the display

A 6000-count display: the biggest number it can show is 5999 (with the decimal point wherever the range puts it). The meter steps to the next range up as a reading passes that.

AUTO

Autorange is on. The meter picks the range and moves the decimal point itself.

0L.

OL, overload. The value is above the range: an open circuit in Ω or continuity, a reversed diode (shown as .0L), or too high in mV. The bar graph fills up with an arrow at the end.

−5.194

The minus sign means polarity is reversed. Swap the probes, or just read it as negative.

HOLD

The reading is frozen. Press H/☀ to release it. A frozen reading isn't live, so don't trust it for safety checks.

▮▮▮▮▮▮▯▯▯▯

Bar graph (0–60 scale, follows the range). It updates faster than the digits, which makes it good for spotting trends, peaks and flicker.

USB

The PC data link is on. Hold REL/USB to toggle it.

m µ n · k M

Unit prefixes: milli (÷1000), micro (÷1,000,000), nano; kilo (×1000), mega (×1,000,000). 4.7 kΩ = 4700 Ω.

DC · AC

DC is steady one-direction voltage or current. AC alternates, like mains. The NK61P reads AC as True RMS, so it stays accurate on non-sine waveforms.

▭ low battery

Replace the battery soon. Readings can be wrong when it's low.

Safety

Measurement categories

The CAT rating says how big a voltage spike the meter can survive. Spikes get bigger the closer you are to the power coming into the building. The NK61P is rated 1000 V CAT III and 600 V CAT IV. Your test leads have their own rating, and the lower of the two applies.

CAT IIOK for this meter

Things that plug into outlets: appliances, power tools, the outlets themselves.

CAT IIIOK up to 1000 V

Building wiring: breaker panels, hard-wired motors, fixed wiring and junction boxes.

CAT IVOK up to 600 V

The service entrance: utility meter, service drop, outdoor lines feeding the building.

Every time

  • Inspect the leads. Cracked insulation or exposed metal means replace them.
  • Live, dead, live: check the meter on a known source before and after you test something you plan to touch.
  • On high voltage, keep one hand away from the work and clip the black lead on first.
  • Don't change the dial while the probes touch a circuit.
  • Remove the leads before opening the battery or fuse cover.
  • Don't use the meter if the case is cracked or wet, or near flammable gas.

Fuses and the quick fuse check

The mA jack has a 600 mA fuse and the 10A jack a 10 A fuse. Replace only with the same rating and type: fast-acting, high-breaking-capacity meter fuses. A glass hardware-store fuse is not a safe substitute.

  1. Set the dial to Ω, red lead in VΩHz.
  2. Touch the red probe tip into the mA jack. A low reading means the fuse is good.
  3. Do the same with the 10A jack. It should read close to 0 Ω.
  4. OL means that fuse is blown.
USB link

Logging to the PC

  1. Plug in the USB cable.
  2. Hold REL/USB until the USB icon shows on the screen. Without it the meter sends nothing.
  3. Double-click NK61P Live on the desktop. The dashboard opens in your browser.
  4. Press Start logging to record every reading (about 5 per second) to a CSV file in the logs folder.
